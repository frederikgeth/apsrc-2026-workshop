Generative AI is rapidly becoming embedded in software- and data-intensive
research, creating new opportunities alongside new integrity risks. Drawing on
the principles of the Australian Code for the Responsible Conduct of Research,
this talk will explore how established practices including verification and
validation, benchmarking, open-source software development, data stewardship,
and transparent workflows provide a foundation for the responsible use of AI in
research. Using examples from energy systems modelling and computational
science, I will discuss practical issues such as AI-assisted software
development, management of sensitive data, public versus private repositories,
software and data licensing, and reproducibility in increasingly automated
research environments. The central argument is that AI does not diminish the
importance of research integrity principles; rather, it makes rigorous
computational research practices more important than ever, while also creating
new opportunities for research translation and impact.
