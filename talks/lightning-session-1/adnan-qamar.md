title: "Model-Based Systems Engineering for Alumina Decarbonisation"

As part of Work Package 4 of the HILT CRC, NEAC is applying a Model-Based
Systems Engineering (MBSE) framework, integrated with Hetero-Functional Graph
Theory (HFGT), to support decarbonisation planning for Australian heavy
industry. The current case study focuses on the Worsley alumina refinery, where
a formally verified systems model has been developed to represent the Bayer
process, including digestion, evaporation, precipitation, calcination, and
associated steam and water networks.

Rather than constructing standalone demand or supply models, the framework
provides an integrated representation of industrial processes and multi-carrier
energy systems within a single optimisation environment. This enables the
evaluation of technology substitution pathways, electrification strategies, and
carbon-price scenarios while preserving system-level operational constraints.

The approach supports identification of least-cost decarbonisation pathways,
quantification of fuel-switching thresholds, and assessment of infrastructure
requirements under alternative transition scenarios. The presentation will
demonstrate how systems engineering methods can enhance transparency,
reproducibility, and cross-sector integration in industrial decarbonisation
studies, and will discuss opportunities for open and interoperable modelling
tools within the broader energy modelling community.
