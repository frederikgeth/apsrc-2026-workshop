We have been working on how to open up energy modelling to social insights and
participatory processes. Much modelling deals with social dimensions of the
energy system (e.g. consumer actions and practices; decisions of individuals
and organisations; equity and the distribution of costs, benefits and risks),
but often through assumptions that are not always supported with social
evidence, nor transparent to the users of modelling outputs. In addition, while
modelling can underpin operational, investment and policy decisions with
wide-reaching social implications, it often excludes input and engagement by
non-experts and the publics it is intended to ultimately benefit.

Our research considers ways to integrate social insights into modelling, using
both quantitative and qualitative approaches, and how to use models to engage
with different stakeholders and publics. We are looking at iterative ways that
use models to communicate about systems with non-experts, and to use non-expert
input to refine models. We will briefly discuss our progress and approaches,
and invite workshop participants to discuss with us how their models could be
used to open up to non-expert participation.
